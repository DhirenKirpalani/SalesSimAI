# Day1 — Scenario Brief (Rep-Facing)

**This is the only context the trainee sees before the call starts.** It's deliberately incomplete — the buyer's real situation is discovered through the call, not handed to you here.

---

## The setup

You're a sales rep at an EOR (Employer of Record) vendor. Three weeks ago you sent a cold email sequence about the cost and complexity of employing people internationally. On the third touch, you got a reply:

> "Open to a short call. Mainly interested in understanding your pricing model and which countries you cover. 30 min max."

That reply booked a 30-minute intro call. This is that call — the buyer's first-ever conversation with your company.

## Who you're meeting

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Name / role | Daniel Ong, Head of Finance & Operations |
| Company | Kestrel Systems — B2B SaaS (logistics analytics), ~45 employees, Series A raised ~14 months ago, HQ Singapore |
| What's public | Company site, funding announcement, LinkedIn headcount trend (roughly flat over the last 6 months), 2 open roles on the job board (1 Singapore, 1 "Remote — APAC") |
| What you don't know yet | Whether he has people outside Singapore today, on what kind of arrangement, why he actually replied, or what would make this call worth his time beyond "pricing and coverage" |

That's it. No further briefing exists — anything else about Daniel's actual situation is something you find out by asking, or you don't find out at all.

## What you're walking into

He booked 30 minutes. He'll treat it as less. He's not hostile, but he's not warm either — he took this call for pricing and coverage information, not a relationship. He has a real reason for taking a cold call from a vendor he doesn't know. Whether you find out what it is, and whether you connect your product to it, is the point of the exercise.

## What "good" looks like

There's no script to follow and no fixed set of questions that guarantees an outcome. Broadly, calls that go well tend to:
- Frame the call so he doesn't feel sold to before he's said anything
- Ask about his actual situation before pitching
- Answer pricing questions with real mechanics, not vague deflections
- Notice if something he says isn't fully answered, and follow it rather than move past it
- Respect it when he declines to go into something — pushing on a closed door costs you, not helps you

There's no guaranteed next step. A call can end with a concrete next meeting, a request for information in writing, or a polite early exit — all three are realistic, valid outcomes depending on how the call goes.
