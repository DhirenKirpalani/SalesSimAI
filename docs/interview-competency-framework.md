# Interview Competency Framework

Used for **First Round Interview** scenario post-call analysis, live coaching, and nudges.

This framework evaluates candidates on 7 behaviorally-anchored competencies observed through the STAR lens (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Unlike generic STAR scoring, this framework focuses on **what** the candidate demonstrates through their examples, not just **how** they structure them.

---

## Competencies

### 1. Strategic Thinking (20%)
> *Does the candidate think in systems, frameworks, and second-order effects?*

A high-scoring candidate frames situations in terms of business impact, identifies root causes rather than surface symptoms, and makes decisions based on structured thinking. They can zoom in on detail and zoom out to context. Watch for: cause-effect reasoning, trade-off awareness, prioritization under constraints.

**Interview signals:** Complexity of the situation they chose, whether their actions were reactive or intentional, how they connected individual actions to larger outcomes.

---

### 2. Autonomy Under Complexity (15%)
> *Can they move forward decisively when the map is incomplete?*

High scorers take ownership in ambiguous situations. They don't wait to be told — they define the problem, build a hypothesis, and act. They describe moments where no one else was steering and they stepped in. Watch for: initiative, self-direction, ownership language ("I decided", "I ran with it", "I figured it out").

**Interview signals:** Whether their stories start with someone else directing them vs. them choosing to act. Whether they describe complexity as a barrier or a context they worked within.

---

### 3. Leverage Without Authority (15%)
> *Can they move people and outcomes they don't control?*

Sales, partnerships, and leadership all require influencing without a direct reporting line. High scorers describe cross-functional wins, stakeholder alignment, and situations where the outcome required someone else to say yes. They show they understand what motivates others and adapt accordingly. Watch for: stakeholder mapping, influence tactics, outcome ownership when others had the power.

**Interview signals:** Whether they just "communicated" or actually changed someone's mind. Whether they took responsibility for the outcome even when they had no formal authority.

---

### 4. Collaboration (15%)
> *Do they make the people around them better?*

High scorers describe situations where the output was better because of how they worked with others. They credit teammates without deflecting accountability. They show conflict as productive friction, not something to avoid. Watch for: "we" balanced with "I", how they handled disagreement, whether they describe outcomes in terms of team impact.

**Interview signals:** Whether their stories are solo achievements dressed up as team plays. Whether conflict resolution shows empathy and pragmatism or just compliance.

---

### 5. Candour and Self-Correction (15%)
> *Are they honest enough about themselves to grow?*

High scorers describe real failures — not safe "I worked too hard" stories. They identify what actually went wrong, including their own role in it. They show they updated their thinking or behavior afterward. This competency separates candidates who can be coached from those who can't. Watch for: specificity of the failure, absence of blame-shifting, evidence of a behavioral change.

**Interview signals:** Whether their failure story makes you wince (good) or sounds rehearsed. Whether they describe what they now do differently with the same specificity as the failure itself.

---

### 6. Counterfeit Recognition (10%)
> *Can they tell the real from the performed?*

High scorers demonstrate healthy skepticism and pattern recognition. They've caught situations where the signal didn't match the reality — a deal that looked closed but wasn't, a stakeholder who said yes but meant maybe, a team dynamic that looked aligned but was simmering. In interviews, they don't give polished answers that ring hollow. They challenge false premises in questions rather than accepting them. Watch for: examples of changed assessments, moments of "I thought X but it was actually Y", willingness to say "I don't know" or "that was wrong."

**Interview signals:** Whether they can describe a moment their instinct was right when the evidence said otherwise. Whether they're performing an interview persona or actually in conversation.

---

### 7. Vision Adjustment (10%)
> *Can they update the destination while keeping the team moving?*

High scorers describe pivots — times when new information required changing course, dropping a prior commitment, or reframing the goal entirely. They don't cling to plans past their usefulness. But they also show they can do this without losing the team or the momentum. Watch for: how they communicated the change, whether they show regret or clarity, how quickly they moved once the old view was obsolete.

**Interview signals:** Whether their pivot stories show courage (they changed before they had to) or just compliance (they changed because they were told to).

---

## Scoring Scale

Each competency is scored 0–100:

| Score | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 0 | No evidence the candidate addressed this competency |
| 20–40 | Vague or surface-level mention — no concrete example |
| 50–70 | Clear example with most elements present — missing depth or specificity |
| 80–100 | Strong, concrete, specific example with a meaningful outcome |

---

## Live Coaching Steps

During the simulation, the live coach tracks which competencies have surfaced and nudges the candidate to demonstrate the ones not yet shown:

| Step | Competency | What to Listen For |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| 0 | Strategic Thinking | Framing, structure, decision-making rationale |
| 1 | Autonomy Under Complexity | Ownership, ambiguity, self-direction |
| 2 | Leverage Without Authority | Influence, cross-functional, stakeholder alignment |
| 3 | Collaboration | Teamwork, conflict, shared outcomes |
| 4 | Candour & Self-Correction | Failures, honest reflection, course correction |
| 5 | Counterfeit Recognition | Pattern recognition, skepticism, changed assessments |
| 6 | Vision Adjustment | Pivots, adapting strategy, updating the plan |
